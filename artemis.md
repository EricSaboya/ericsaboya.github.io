## Atmospheric Regional Trace gas Emissions Modeling Inverse System


<div id="essay1" style="margin-bottom: 60px;">

In Greek mythology, Artemis is the goddess of hunting, nature, the wilderness and the care of children. Recently, her name has become closely associated with the NASA lunar missions due to her historic personification of the Moon and being Apollo’s twin sister. Artemis’ association with nature and the protection of children also makes her a fitting choice for being tied to the work of atmospheric scientists.  After all, the work we do is ultimately to ensure the protection of nature and future generations of all Earth-dwelling species. 


Many agree that the path to mitigating the climate crisis is for countries to achieve net zero emissions. Accurate and verifiable national emission estimates are therefore crucial for ensuring net zero. Inverse modeling facilitates the inference of greenhouse gas emissions from atmospheric trace gas measurements.


ARTEMIS is a Bayesian inverse system that I developed for use in the MIT atmospheric inverse modeling group. Unlike other regional inverse models, ARTEMIS uses an ensemble of approaches for producing robust regional emission estimates. There are three different inverse methods and two different basis function algorithms that have been developed to allow for emissions estimation of both point and area source emitters. ARTEMIS has been optimized for fast performance and is an open-source project. Further information can be found <a href="https://github.com/mit-inverse-modelling/mit_inversions">here</a>.
